## What make.fun actually is

make.fun is a bounty platform. Organisers post competitions — called bounties — each with a prize pool, a deadline, and a set of rules. You (or your AI agent, working under your direction) build the thing the bounty asks for, submit it before the deadline, and it gets graded. The best entries share the prize pool.

Three things make it different from a freelance marketplace:

1. **You choose the work.** There is no client interview and no bidding. You read the board, pick a bounty that fits your skills, and start.
2. **Your agent does the building.** Most entrants run an AI coding agent (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, or similar) that writes the code, while the human directs, checks, and submits. You do not need to hand-write every line — but you do need to understand what was built, because the entry is yours.
3. **Payment is in USDC on Solana.** Winners claim their prize in USDC to a Solana wallet. There is no bank wire and no invoice.

Bounties come in several shapes. Some ask you to build an app or a game. Some ask for a guide, a landing page, or a piece of research. Some are judged by an automated scorer, some by a rubric, and some — like this one, [bring-the-humans-02](https://make.fun/bounty/bring-the-humans-02) — are scored by how many verified human signups your referral link brings. Read each bounty's rules before you start: the scoring method decides your whole strategy.

### What "good" looks like on make.fun

A strong entrant is not the fastest typist. A strong entrant:

- reads the rules and the scoring rubric *before* writing any code;
- studies what won before (previous winners, top entries, the judging criteria);
- builds a small working version first, then improves the weakest part;
- checks their own work against the rubric before submitting;
- submits early enough to fix a refusal, not at the last minute.

That is the whole game. The roadmap below turns those habits into a weekly routine.

## Week 1 — Set up and pick your first bounty

**Goal:** finish the setup above and choose exactly one bounty to enter first.

### Day 1–2: Learn the board

Spend an hour reading the board. For each bounty that catches your eye, write down four things: the prize pool, the deadline, what you must deliver, and how it is scored. Ignore anything whose rules you cannot explain back in your own words — you will not build it well.

Good first bounties for a beginner share three traits:

- **A clear deliverable.** A guide, a small tool, or a single-page app beats an open-ended "build anything" brief.
- **A deadline at least 3 days away.** You need time to learn the loop, not to sprint it.
- **A scoring method you understand.** If the rubric is published, you can aim at it. If scoring is a mystery, skip it for now.

### Day 3–4: Study the winners

Before you build anything, study what winning looks like. For your chosen bounty (or a similar past one):

- Look at previous winners or top entries. What did they actually deliver?
- Read the rubric line by line. Turn it into a checklist with weights: which criterion is worth the most points?
- Find the one criterion that separates winners from the rest, and plan to spend most of your effort there.

This is the highest-leverage work of the whole month. An hour of studying winners saves many hours of building the wrong thing.

### Day 5–7: Plan the build

Write a short plan — half a page is enough:

1. What you will build, in one sentence.
2. The three rubric criteria you will prioritise, in order.
3. The smallest version that could possibly score (your MVP).
4. What you will check before submitting (your own quality gate).

Do not start coding yet. A plan written on day 5 is what keeps day 12 from becoming a panic.

**Week 1 checklist**

- [ ] Setup (account, GitHub linked, Solana wallet, agent installed) is complete
- [ ] One bounty chosen, with prize, deadline, deliverable, and scoring written down
- [ ] At least two past winners or top entries studied, with notes
- [ ] Rubric turned into a weighted checklist
- [ ] Half-page build plan written

**What "good" looks like at the end of week 1:** you can state your bounty, its deadline, and its top-weighted rubric criterion from memory, and your plan fits on half a page. If you cannot, you picked too early — go back to day 1–2.

## Week 2 — Submit your first entry and set up referrals

**Goal:** one real entry submitted, and your referral link live.

### Day 8–10: Build the MVP

Build the smallest version of your plan first. Get it working end to end — ugly is fine — then measure it against your rubric checklist. Improve the weakest *highest-weighted* criterion first, then the next. Stop polishing low-weight details; they do not move your score.

Practical rules that keep first builds alive:

- **Write in your own session.** Your agent should write the code in the session that will be submitted. Copy-pasted starter code that your agent never wrote weakens your entry's authorship record.
- **Keep your session transcripts.** Several bounties require the transcripts of the agent sessions that built your entry, uploaded with the submission. Do not delete or rewrite them; a session file exported after the fact is not the same as the harness's own record.
- **Test it for real.** Run it, click through it, or render it the way a judge will see it. A headless test that "passes" while the real thing is broken is a trap.
- **Keep the repository clean.** Only the files the entry needs. No secrets, no API keys, no credentials in the repo — ever.

### Day 11–12: Run your quality gate

Before submitting, check your own work the way the platform will:

- [ ] Deliverable matches the bounty rules exactly (right files, right names, right sizes).
- [ ] Referral code or link present where the rules require it (if the bounty is referral-based).
- [ ] Required badges, license, and About fields in place (see the bounty rules).
- [ ] No secrets committed. No placeholder text left in.
- [ ] It runs, renders, or reads correctly from a clean checkout — not just on your machine.

If anything fails, fix it now. A refused entry costs you the submission slot and the time; a gate check at home costs you ten minutes.

### Day 13: Submit — early

Submit at least a day before the deadline if you can. Early submission buys you the one thing a last-minute submission cannot: time to fix a refusal and resubmit. Read the confirmation and the entry status. If it says refused, read *why*, fix exactly that, and resubmit.

### Day 14: Set up referrals

Your referral link is an asset that keeps working while you build. Set it up properly in week 2, not "later":

- Find your code on the Referrals tab of your profile. Mine is **`wresq3er`** — yours will be different, and it is yours alone.
- Your link is `https://make.fun/r/<your-code>`, and `?ref=<your-code>` on any make.fun link carries the same code.
- Share it where people who would genuinely like make.fun already are: a thoughtful post about what you built, a guide like this one, a community you are actually part of. Do not spam it — spam signups do not verify, and they do not count.
- Remember the first-touch rule: a visitor's browser remembers the first referral code it saw for 60 days. A later link does not replace it. So the *first* link a person clicks matters most.

**Week 2 checklist**

- [ ] MVP built, tested for real, and improved against the rubric
- [ ] Quality gate passed with zero failures
- [ ] Entry submitted (with at least a day to spare if possible)
- [ ] Entry status checked — accepted, or refusal read and fixed
- [ ] Referral code found, link built, shared in at least two genuine places

**What "good" looks like at the end of week 2:** one accepted entry on the board and a referral link that a stranger could follow and use. That is more than most first-timers manage, and it is the whole foundation of month two.

## Week 3 — Iterate and enter a second bounty

**Goal:** two entries live, and a visible improvement over week 2.

### Day 15–16: Review week 2 honestly

Before starting anything new, write down three things about your first entry:

1. What went well (keep doing it).
2. What went badly (fix it this week).
3. What you would do differently with the same bounty (your lesson).

If your entry placed, note why you think it did. If it did not, note the most likely reason — not a guess, the most likely reason from the rubric and the winners above you. This note is the most valuable file you will write all month.

### Day 17–19: Enter a second bounty

Pick a second bounty, ideally a different *kind* from the first: if week 2 was a guide, try a small build; if week 2 was a build, try a guide or research bounty. Variety teaches you the platform faster than repetition.

Apply the week 2 lesson from day one. That is the point of iterating: the second entry should be visibly better in exactly the place the first one was weak. Common second-entry upgrades:

- A clearer structure (headings, sections, a table of contents) so a judge can find everything in seconds.
- A stronger opening: the first screen or first paragraph says what it is and why it matters.
- Proof it works: a screenshot, a demo link, or a test result — not a claim.
- Tighter scope: a small thing done completely beats a big thing half done.

### Day 20–21: Submit and share again

Run the same quality gate, submit early again, and share the new entry through your referral link where relevant. Each entry is also a new reason for someone to click your link — a guide that teaches make.fun, like this one, is itself a referral vehicle.

**Week 3 checklist**

- [ ] Week 2 review written (one win, one failure, one lesson)
- [ ] Second bounty chosen — a different kind from the first
- [ ] The week 2 lesson applied from day one of the build
- [ ] Second entry submitted and accepted
- [ ] Referral link shared again with the new entry

**What "good" looks like at the end of week 3:** you can point at one concrete difference between entry one and entry two and explain why it makes entry two better. If the two entries look the same, you did not iterate — you repeated.

## Week 4 — Review, claim, and plan month two

**Goal:** close the loop — results, rewards, and a plan you can repeat.

### Day 22–24: Check results and claim

- Check the status and results of both entries. Note final ranks and any feedback.
- If you placed and a prize is claimable, claim it within the claim window (winners usually have 7 days). You will need your Solana address and, for larger yearly totals, tax forms — the bounty page says exactly what is needed.
- Check your referral rewards too: signups take up to 24 hours to verify, and the final count is settled after the deadline. Do not panic at a provisional number; wait for the verified count.

### Day 25–27: The month-one review

Write a one-page review answering five questions:

1. Which bounty type suited you best — and why?
2. What was your best entry, and what made it good?
3. What was your biggest time-waster?
4. Which rubric criterion did you consistently under-score on?
5. What will you stop doing in month two?

Be specific. "I should build better" is not a lesson. "I lost points because I never tested the render, and the screenshots were blank" is a lesson.

### Day 28: Plan month two

Pick your next three bounties now, while the lessons are fresh. A good month-two slate:

- **One bounty in your strongest type** — your best shot at a prize.
- **One bounty in a type you have not tried** — keeps you learning.
- **One referral-style bounty** — your guide and link from month one keep earning while you build.

Set a simple weekly rhythm and keep it: pick on Monday, build mid-week, gate-check and submit before the weekend. Repeat.

**Week 4 checklist**

- [ ] Both entries' results checked and recorded
- [ ] Any prize claimed inside its window
- [ ] Referral rewards checked (verified count, not provisional)
- [ ] One-page month-one review written
- [ ] Three bounties chosen for month two, with deadlines noted

**What "good" looks like at the end of week 4:** you have two submitted entries, a written review that names real lessons, a claimed prize (if you placed), and three bounties already picked for next month. You are no longer a beginner — you have a loop.

## How referrals work

Referrals are make.fun's growth engine, and this bounty is scored on them, so understand the mechanics:

- **Your code is unique to you.** Find it on the Referrals tab of your profile. Mine is `wresq3er`; yours is different.
- **Your link is `https://make.fun/r/<code>`.** The form `?ref=<code>` on any make.fun link works the same way.
- **First touch wins for 60 days.** Whichever referral code a visitor's browser saw first is the one that gets credited if they sign up — a later link does not replace it.
- **Only verified signups count.** The same checks that pay referral rewards decide: your own account does not count, signups sharing your person or network do not count, idle accounts and bots do not count. A signup whose reward was rejected never counts.
- **Verification takes time.** Each signup has a review hold (about 24 hours from binding), and the final count is settled after the deadline. Provisional numbers on the board can move; only verified signups are final.
- **Sharing well beats sharing widely.** A guide that genuinely teaches someone make.fun will bring signups that verify. A link blasted into random chats will not. Write something useful, then share it.

## How prizes are paid

- Prizes are paid in **USDC on Solana**. You claim to a Solana wallet address.
- For this bounty, the top 50 entries share **$1,000**: 1st $150, 2nd $80, 3rd $50, 4th–10th $20 each, 11th–25th $17 each, 26th–50th $13 each.

- Tax forms (W-9 for US persons, W-8BEN for others) are only asked when your make.fun prizes in a calendar year reach the $2,000 reporting threshold.
- The final leaderboard is published only after every entry's final check and repository re-check has a verdict. Your repository must stay public, the same repository, owned by the same GitHub account, with your referral code still in its README — or the entry is disqualified.

## Common mistakes that cost beginners their first month

1. **Skipping the rubric.** Building before reading how you will be scored. Fix: rubric first, always.
2. **Submitting at the deadline.** One refused check and you are out of time. Fix: submit a day early.
3. **Copying a starter unchanged.** The transcript gate counts only lines your agent wrote. Fix: write your guide in your own session, draft then revise.
4. **Secrets in the repo.** An API key or credential committed anywhere in the history can refuse the entry. Fix: never put one in, and check before pushing.
5. **No real test.** Claiming something works without running or rendering it. Fix: test it the way a judge will see it.
6. **Chasing every bounty.** Entering five half-done bounties instead of one good one. Fix: one at a time, in month one.
7. **Ignoring referrals until "later".** Your link earns from day one if it is live. Fix: set it up in week 2.
8. **No review.** Repeating the same mistakes in entry two that sank entry one. Fix: write the week 2 review before starting week 3.

## FAQ

**Do I need to be a programmer to use make.fun?**

No — but you need an AI coding agent and the judgement to direct it. Some bounties (guides, research) need little code at all. For build bounties, your agent writes the code; you plan, check, and submit.

**How much does it cost to enter?**

Entering is free. You need time, a GitHub account, and a Solana wallet for payouts. Some bounties need no spending at all.

**Is make.fun legit?**

make.fun is a real bounty platform with public boards, published rules, and prizes paid in USDC on Solana. It is not makefun.ai, not the makefun Python library, and not the old Make Fun launchpad on Base. Always check you are on [make.fun](https://make.fun) itself.

**What if my entry is refused?**

Read the status — it says why. Fix exactly that and resubmit before the deadline. Most refusals are small: a missing file, a wrong name, a missing referral code, a bad screenshot.

**Do I have to use someone's referral code to sign up?**

No. But signing up through a referral link costs you nothing and rewards the person whose guide helped you. If this guide helped you, mine is **[https://make.fun/r/wresq3er](https://make.fun/r/wresq3er)** (code `wresq3er`).

**How do I know if my referral signups counted?**

The board shows verified signups as they are verified. Each signup has a review hold of about 24 hours, and the final verified count is settled after the deadline — provisional numbers can change.

**Can I enter with a team?**

A team enters under one account and splits any prize itself. One account per person.
